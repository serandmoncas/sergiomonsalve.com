Test yourself · retrieval practice

Myth or evidence?

Twenty widely repeated ideas about how we learn. Decide before you see the answer: trying to recall and getting it wrong helps more than reading the answer. The page remembers your answers in this browser and asks you again, days later, what you got wrong and what you have not reviewed in a while. That is how spaced review works.

Plan your review · spaced practice

When should you review for an exam?

Spreading study over several days pays off more than cramming the night before. Enter the exam date and the planner suggests short sessions, further and further apart, where you test yourself without looking.

Each session, 20 to 30 minutes. Close the notebook, write or say out loud what you remember, and then check what you missed.

    How the brain learns

    Remembering, practicing and understanding

    2014Solid

    Make It Stick

    Author
    Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III y Mark A. McDaniel

    Connects with

    • Ultralearning · Make It Stick is the science behind two principles of Ultralearning, retrieval and retention.
    • Learning How to Learn · The same ideas about how we learn, one version for adults and another for children and teenagers.

    The promise: Recalling without looking teaches more than rereading.

    Recalling what you studied without looking beat rereading it, and also beat making concept maps.

    Roediger y Karpicke, 2006 · doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2018Partly right

    Learning How to Learn

    Author
    Barbara Oakley y Terrence Sejnowski, con Alistair McConville

    Connects with

    • Deep Work · Two ways of focusing. Short 25-minute blocks with breaks (Oakley) or long blocks without distractions (Newport).

    The promise: Letting a problem rest helps you solve it.

    It has support, especially for creative problems. Other tools in the book, such as the Pomodoro technique, have no studies of their own.

    Sio y Ormerod, 2009 · doi.org/10.1037/a0014212

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2011Solid

    Moonwalking with Einstein

    Author
    Joshua Foer

    Connects with

    • Limitless · The memory palace. Kwik teaches it as a technique; Foer tells how he learned it and how far it goes.

    The promise: An exceptional memory is a technique, not a gift.

    People with no experience who trained for six weeks with the memory palace improved, and the gain was still there four months later. It works for lists, not for understanding a subject.

    Dresler y otros, 2017 · doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.02.003

    Practice, talent and mindset

    How much is innate and how much is effort

    2016Partly right

    Peak

    Author
    Anders Ericsson y Robert Pool

    Connects with

    • Outliers · The 10,000-hour rule. Gladwell popularized it with Ericsson's study, and Ericsson replies that this is not what he found.
    • Moonwalking with Einstein · Foer uses Ericsson's deliberate practice to get out of the plateau where his memory had stopped improving.

    The promise: Experts are made through deliberate practice.

    Practice matters, but it explains less than the book suggests: 26 % of the differences in games such as chess and 4 % in education.

    Macnamara, Hambrick y Oswald, 2014 · doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810

    Outliers

    Author
    Malcolm Gladwell

    The promise: The 10,000-hour rule.

    The birth-date effect is solid, but the 10,000-hour rule is not: practice explains less than 1 % of the differences in professions.

    Macnamara, Hambrick y Oswald, 2014 · doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2006Promises more than it proves

    Mindset

    Author
    Carol S. Dweck

    Connects with

    • Limitless · Both say beliefs about ourselves limit what we learn. Dweck has more studies behind her, and even so her effect is small.
    • Outliers · Dweck looks at what happens inside the person (their mindset); Gladwell, at what happens outside (their opportunities).

    The promise: Believing you can improve raises your grades.

    In the highest-quality studies, the average effect of mindset interventions is close to zero.

    Macnamara y Burgoyne, 2023 · doi.org/10.1037/bul0000352

    Habits and focus

    What we do without thinking, and how to protect attention

    2018Partly right

    Atomic Habits

    Author
    James Clear

    Connects with

    • The Odyssey · Designing your surroundings so you do not give in to temptation is the modern version of tying yourself to the mast.

    The promise: Decide when and where you will do something.

    Implementation intentions had a medium-to-large effect across 94 studies. The idea of changing your identity, on the other hand, has not been tested.

    Gollwitzer y Sheeran, 2006 · doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1

    Deep Work

    Author
    Cal Newport

    Connects with

    • Ultralearning · Focus is one of the nine principles of Ultralearning; Newport devotes a whole book to it.

    The promise: Technology ruins young people's focus.

    In more than 350,000 teenagers, digital technology use had a very small relationship with their well-being. The problem exists, but the book enlarges it.

    Orben y Przybylski, 2019 · doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0506-1

    Learning on your own

    Methods for self-learners, and their promises

    2019Partly right

    Ultralearning

    Author
    Scott H. Young

    Connects with

    • Limitless · Two promises of faster learning. Comparing them is a good exercise in separating what has evidence from what is merely said.
    • The Art of Thinking Clearly · Ultralearning relies on stories of people who succeeded; Dobelli explains why that can be survivorship bias.
    • Robinson Crusoe · Learning alone and by doing. Robinson learns to bake bread and make pots through failed attempts.

    The promise: Learn fast and intensely.

    Reviewing spaced out over time beats studying everything at once, which clashes with the intensity the book proposes.

    Cepeda y otros, 2006 · doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2020Promises more than it proves

    Limitless

    Author
    Jim Kwik

    Connects with

    • The Art of Thinking Clearly · Dobelli teaches you to distrust good stories and authority, useful for reading with a cool head any book that promises to change your life, this one included.

    The promise: You can read much faster without understanding less.

    The most complete review concludes that you cannot: when reading speeds up, comprehension drops.

    Rayner y otros, 2016 · doi.org/10.1177/1529100615623267

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2011Partly right

    The Art of Thinking Clearly

    Author
    Rolf Dobelli

    Connects with

    • Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds · Dobelli explains social proof in a chapter; Mackay shows whole countries falling for it.
    • The Odyssey · Hyperbolic discounting (preferring today's reward) is the problem; tying yourself to the mast is a solution.
    • Make It Stick · Overconfidence, student version. Rereading gives the illusion of knowing, and Make It Stick shows how to check it.
    • Atomic Habits · Dobelli says willpower runs out; Clear proposes not depending on it and changing your surroundings instead.

    The promise: Knowing your biases protects you from them.

    Reading about biases helps little. Practicing with cases and getting feedback did reduce them, and the effect lasted two months.

    Morewedge y otros, 2015 · doi.org/10.1177/2372732215600886

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    Pedagogy

    What teaching is and what learning is for

    1970Philosophy of education

    Pedagogy of the Oppressed

    Author
    Paulo Freire

    Connects with

    • The Ignorant Schoolmaster · Two critiques of the teacher who only deposits or explains. Freire proposes dialogue; Rancière, the equality of intelligences.

    The promise: Teach through dialogue, not deposits.

    It is philosophy and politics of education. Classroom dialogue has support: in 72 classrooms, broad participation was associated with better mastery of the content.

    Howe y otros, 2019 · doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2019.1573730

    1987Philosophy of education

    The Ignorant Schoolmaster

    Author
    Jacques Rancière

    Connects with

    • Ultralearning · Learning without anyone explaining. Jacotot in 1818, and Scott Young with MIT's open courses.

    The promise: You can teach what you do not know.

    It is philosophy. For beginners, the evidence favors explicit instruction and worked examples over discovering alone.

    Kirschner, Sweller y Clark, 2006 · doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1

    1762Philosophy of education

    Emile, or On Education

    Author
    Jean-Jacques Rousseau

    Connects with

    • Robinson Crusoe · It is the first book Rousseau lets Emile read, the model of someone who learns from things and not from speeches.
    • Pedagogy of the Oppressed · Two roots of active education, two centuries apart. Learning from experience (Rousseau) and from dialogue about one's own reality (Freire).

    The promise: A child is not a small adult.

    It is an imaginary experiment from 1762, not research. Its value lies in the questions it opened.

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    1960Philosophy of education

    Summerhill

    Author
    A. S. Neill

    Connects with

    • Emile, or On Education · Both start from the idea that children are good by nature and learn best in freedom. Neill took it to a real school.
    • The Ignorant Schoolmaster · Trusting that students can learn by themselves. Jacotot without explaining, Neill without compulsory classes.
    • El abordaje de la educación experiencial (in Spanish only) · Challenge by choice and Summerhill's optional classes share one idea, that nobody learns by force.

    The promise: A school where classes are optional.

    It has been open for more than a hundred years. Graduates of another democratic school went on to study or work without major difficulties, although families choose the school.

    Gray y Chanoff, 1986 · doi.org/10.1086/443842

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2003Partly right

    El abordaje de la educación experiencial (in Spanish only)

    Author
    Juan Felipe Builes Jaramillo

    Connects with

    • Emile, or On Education · Rousseau is among the philosophical foundations of experiential education, learning from things and not from speeches.
    • Pedagogy of the Oppressed · Both start from John Dewey. Freire takes him to adult literacy and social justice; Builes, to group adventure workshops.
    • Make It Stick · They agree that living through something is not enough to learn it. You need to reflect (Builes) or retrieve from memory (Make It Stick).

    The promise: Learning by doing and reflecting.

    Adventure programs have moderate positive effects that keep growing afterwards, but they vary a lot between programs.

    Hattie y otros, 1997 · doi.org/10.3102/00346543067001043
