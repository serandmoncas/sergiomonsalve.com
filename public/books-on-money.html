Test yourself · retrieval practice

Myth or evidence?

Ten ideas about money and how we handle it. Decide before you see the answer: trying to recall and getting it wrong helps you learn more than reading the answer. The page remembers your answers in this browser and asks you again, days later, what you got wrong.

Plan your review · spaced practice

When should you review for an exam?

Spreading study over several days pays off more than cramming the night before. Enter the exam date and the planner suggests short sessions, further and further apart, where you test yourself without looking.

Each session, 20 to 30 minutes. Close the notebook, write or say out loud what you remember, and then check what you missed.

    The history of money and markets

    Where accounting, trade and bubbles come from

    1202Literature and history

    Liber Abaci

    Author
    Leonardo de Pisa (Fibonacci)
    Lessons
    The real cost of a loan

    Connects with

    • The Merchant of Venice · Italian merchants and loans. Fibonacci taught them to do the sums; Shakespeare showed what happens when a loan goes wrong.
    • Essay on the Nature of Trade in General · Two books written for merchants, five centuries apart.

    The promise: The numbers we do our sums with today.

    It helped Europe leave Roman numerals behind, and it shows that the mathematics of credit is more than 800 years old.

    Essay on the Nature of Trade in General

    Author
    Richard Cantillon
    Lessons
    Starting a business with numbers, Inflation

    Connects with

    • Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds · Cantillon got rich from the John Law bubble that Mackay tells about, and sold before it burst.

    The promise: The entrepreneur buys at a certain price to sell at an uncertain one.

    It defines the entrepreneur as the one who takes on risk, and explains why, when new money is created, those who receive it last pay higher prices.

    How we decide with money

    Predictable mistakes, and how to design better decisions

    2011Partly right

    Thinking, Fast and Slow

    Author
    Daniel Kahneman
    Lessons
    Scams and pyramid schemes, Investing, the basics, The real cost of a loan, Insurance

    Connects with

    • The Art of Thinking Clearly · Dobelli lists thinking errors; Kahneman explains where they come from, with the theory of two systems.
    • Nudge · Kahneman describes how the mind goes wrong; Thaler and Sunstein, how to design decisions so those errors weigh less.
    • Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds · The bubbles Mackay tells about are overconfidence and social proof on the scale of a country.

    The promise: We think with a fast system and a slow one.

    The book offers a serious theory and much of it holds, such as the anchoring effect. But a famous experiment in chapter 4, in which reading words about old age made people walk more slowly, could not be repeated.

    Doyen y otros, 2012 · doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0029081

    2015Partly right

    Misbehaving

    Author
    Richard H. Thaler
    Lessons
    Budget, Saving and goals, Need or want?, Investing, the basics

    Connects with

    • Thinking, Fast and Slow · The same story seen from two sides, Kahneman from psychology and Thaler from economics. They worked together.
    • Nudge · Misbehaving tells how the idea of nudges was born and how it reached governments.
    • Pequeño cerdo capitalista · Macías teaches you to see small daily expenses; Thaler explains why they slip away, because we do not treat all money the same, it depends on the mental drawer we put it in.

    The promise: People make predictable mistakes with money.

    Those given a mug asked much more to sell it than others offered to buy it. It is the endowment effect; with careful training on the rules of the experiment, the gap can disappear.

    Kahneman, Knetsch y Thaler, 1990 · doi.org/10.1086/261737

    2008Partly right

    Nudge

    Author
    Richard H. Thaler y Cass R. Sunstein
    Lessons
    Saving and goals, Budget, Credit cards

    Connects with

    • Atomic Habits · Both say surroundings matter more than willpower. Clear applies it to yourself; Thaler and Sunstein, to whoever designs other people's choices.
    • The Odyssey · Save More Tomorrow is tying yourself to the mast, committing today to save the pay raises still to come.
    • Pequeño cerdo capitalista · Saving automatically, so you do not depend on remembering or on willpower.

    The promise: A well-designed nudge changes what people choose.

    Default saving works very well. But after correcting for unpublished studies, the average effect of nudges almost disappears. Some work a lot and others hardly at all.

    Maier y otros, 2022 · doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200300119

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2012Solid

    The Signal and the Noise

    Author
    Nate Silver
    Lessons
    Investing, the basics, Scams and pyramid schemes

    Connects with

    • Algorithms to Live By · Both teach Bayes' rule and overfitting, updating what we believe with each new piece of data without mistaking noise for signal.
    • Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds · Mackay tells of the 18th-century bubbles; Silver, of the 2008 one, and why almost nobody saw it coming.
    • Thinking, Fast and Slow · Experts are wrong with great confidence. Kahneman explains why; Silver shows how to forecast better.

    The promise: Forecasting better can be learned.

    In a two-year forecasting tournament, training in probability, working in teams and grouping the best forecasters improved the forecasts.

    Mellers y otros, 2014 · doi.org/10.1177/0956797614524255

    Saving, starting a business and living well

    From the budget to your own business, and how much money matters in a good life

    2011Partly right

    Pequeño cerdo capitalista

    Author
    Sofía Macías
    Lessons
    Income and expenses, Budget, Saving and goals, Good debt and bad debt, Credit cards, Investing, the basics

    Connects with

    • Aesop's Fables · Small daily expenses and the ant in the fable, two ways of explaining that small things, repeated, become big.
    • David Copperfield · Micawber's rule and Macías's budget say the same thing 160 years apart, spend a little less than what comes in.
    • Atomic Habits · Saving as a small, automatic habit, not as a heroic effort.

    The promise: Small daily expenses eat your money.

    It has support, a cost presented per day seems smaller than it is. But the book thinks of Mexico and of people with some margin, and small expenses do not explain a lack of money when income is not enough.

    Gourville, 1998 · doi.org/10.1086/209517

    2011Partly right

    The Lean Startup

    Author
    Eric Ries
    Lessons
    Starting a business with numbers, Self-employment

    Connects with

    • Designing Your Life · The same idea applied to different things, testing small before deciding. Ries with a business, Burnett and Evans with your own life.
    • The Signal and the Noise · Both ask you to treat what you believe as a hypothesis and change your mind when the data says so.
    • Pequeño cerdo capitalista · Macías teaches you to look after your own money; Ries, not to spend it on a business before knowing if anyone wants it.

    The promise: Test a business idea before investing in it.

    In a trial with 116 startups assigned at random, those who learned to test hypotheses performed better. The method grew out of Silicon Valley anecdotes and does not work equally for every business.

    Camuffo y otros, 2020 · doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249

    Read the full review (in Spanish)

    2023Partly right

    The Good Life

    Author
    Robert Waldinger y Marc Schulz
    Lessons
    Need or want?

    Connects with

    • Man's Search for Meaning · Frankl held on by thinking of his wife; 85 years of data say relationships are what matters most in a good life.
    • Nonviolent Communication · Waldinger says relationships are cared for like health; Rosenberg gives a concrete way to do it when there is conflict.
    • Pequeño cerdo capitalista · Money does matter for well-being, but it does not replace friends. A good pair for the Need or want? lesson.

    The promise: Relationships matter more than money in a good life.

    Relationships are associated with more health and happiness, although that does not prove they cause them. And money does matter, well-being keeps rising with income for most people.

    Killingsworth, Kahneman y Mellers, 2023 · doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208661120
