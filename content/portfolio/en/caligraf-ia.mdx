## The problem

Practicing calligraphy on your own has two gaps: getting practice sheets in the style you want, and knowing what you're doing wrong without a teacher beside you. The idea came up in the Experiencias IA study group, when a fellow member said they wanted to get back into calligraphy.

## The solution

Caligraf IA combines two things:

- **Printable practice sheets (PDF)** in eye-catching pen styles —italic, blackletter, copperplate— plus brush lettering. Anyone can generate them.
- **A photo correction loop:** you practice on paper, take a photo, and the AI returns at most three concrete observations (letter, aspect, explanation), something that's going well, and the letters to reinforce in the next sheet.

It's free and open, but AI correction requires an access request that an admin can approve or revoke, with an adjustable weekly cap to keep costs in check.

## Technical decisions

**The AI never draws letters.** Sheets are built from real, openly licensed fonts and vector guides; the AI only decides what to practice. A model that invents strokes teaches badly; a well-made font doesn't.

**Measure before building.** Before building the app, the first plan is a precision test: 15 to 20 real photos per style, analyzed by Claude and graded by a calligrapher as correct, wrong or invented. A style only ships with correction if the AI gets at least 80% of observations right and invents fewer than 5%; otherwise it ships with practice sheets only.

**Structured output and AI honesty.** The analysis uses the Claude API with output validated by a Zod schema: it first decides whether the photo is legible (if not, it asks for another one instead of inventing defects), and the three-observation cap is enforced in code, not just in the prompt. Photos are normalized before sending (EXIF orientation, max size, white background for transparent PNGs).

**Spec-driven development with agents.** Spec via brainstorming, a test-first (TDD) plan, and a final whole-branch review, all with Claude Code.

## Results

Just getting started. The spec is approved and the first plan —the precision-test tool— is implemented with 42 automated tests: it analyzes the photos, produces a report for the calligrapher, and summarizes per style whether correction is reliable. The next step is running it on real photos; the app's plans (practice sheets, accounts and access, and in-app correction) depend on those results.
