## The problem

In Colombia, removing the guard from the front gate has become a topic at residents' assemblies. A 24-hour security post costs about COP 19.6 million a month, and on the other side there are offers of virtual concierge, AI cameras and remote monitoring centers for a fraction of that. Almost all the available information comes from the people selling those solutions.

I started with a personal question: whether I could build my own security cameras with an ESP32. That question led to a bigger one about what's real in AI-assisted security and where the sector is heading.

## The research

I did it in two rounds, each coordinated with Claude Code as several parallel lines of research, with per-source notes and a report that cross-checks them:

- **Part 1, for people who buy security** — five lines: technology, evidence of effectiveness, Colombian regulation, market and business, and implementation by site type. Conclusion: AI detects and verifies, but someone still has to show up.
- **Part 2, for people who run a security company** — four lines: international trajectory, transformation of traditional companies, drivers of change in Colombia from 2026 to 2030, and new business models. Conclusion: AI won't replace the guard; it will replace the company that only sells guard hours.

## Method decisions

- **Separate evidence from marketing.** Every figure is tagged by origin: independent study, company, vendor or our own estimate.
- **Say what isn't known.** Each part ends with its limits: there is no independent study comparing remote monitoring with an on-site guard at a private site.
- **No naming or judging Colombian vendors**, and not presented as legal advice.
- **Declared co-authorship.** Claude wrote the drafts from the reports; I set the scope, reviewed and edited.

## What's next

The research feeds the design of **GuardIA**, my self-hosted home video surveillance system with ESP32 cameras and local detection. Its main lesson for that project: a camera that detects well isn't enough; the system has to account for who receives the alert, how it gets verified and what happens next.
